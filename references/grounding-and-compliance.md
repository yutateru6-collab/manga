# Grounding and Compliance

## Fact ledger

Before concepting, maintain a table:

| Claim / asset | Status | Source | Allowed in ad? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Statuses:

- `VERIFIED`
- `USER-PROVIDED`
- `UNKNOWN`
- `INFERENCE`

## Never invent

Do not fabricate:

- customer quotes or reviews
- star ratings
- user counts
- download counts
- prices or discounts
- deadlines or scarcity
- awards/certifications
- test scores or measurable outcomes
- medical/financial/educational guarantees
- competitor limitations
- screenshots or UI behaviors represented as real

If a fictional placeholder is useful during concept development, label it clearly as fictional and keep it out of a final ad.

## Proof hierarchy

Prefer stronger proof when available:

1. direct product demo / accurate screenshot
2. measured product analytics or documented result
3. real case study
4. real customer language/testimonial with permission as required
5. verifiable feature/differentiator
6. founder explanation of why/how the product was built

Generic praise is weak proof even when true.

## Comparative advertising

Comparisons must be specific and defensible. “Old way vs new way” is often safer than naming a competitor unless the comparison is current, accurate, and appropriate for the platform/jurisdiction.

## Before/after

Before/after framing can imply typical results. Make the states honest and comparable. Do not exaggerate outcomes or visually manipulate evidence.

## Urgency and scarcity

Only use urgency when a real time/quantity constraint exists. Never invent countdowns, “last chance,” low stock, or limited seats.

## UI and chat simulations

Clearly distinguish a dramatized scenario from a real testimonial, real customer chat, or real system response. Do not forge native UI screenshots to imply third-party endorsement or real user behavior.

## Regulated/high-stakes claims

For health, finance, legal, political, employment, housing, or other regulated/high-stakes advertising, require stronger source verification and current policy/legal review. This skill is not a substitute for legal review.

## Copyright / likeness / brand handling

- Do not imitate a living artist's exact style; describe broader visual characteristics.
- Do not use third-party logos, characters, or celebrity likenesses as implied endorsement without rights/permission.
- Keep product/brand assets faithful to supplied sources.
