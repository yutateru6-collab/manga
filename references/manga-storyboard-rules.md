# Manga Storyboard Rules

## Panel jobs

Every panel should have one dominant job:

- stop attention
- establish problem
- escalate friction
- reveal mechanism
- demonstrate product
- show proof
- resolve emotion
- ask for action

If a panel has three jobs, split or simplify it.

## Useful advertising story shapes

### A. Recognition → Discovery
1. recognizable pain
2. pain becomes inconvenient/costly
3. product appears naturally
4. mechanism/demo
5. specific payoff
6. CTA

### B. Old way → New way
1. old workflow in action
2. frustration or waste becomes visible
3. new workflow/product introduced
4. side-by-side mechanism difference
5. honest result
6. CTA

### C. Objection → Evidence
1. audience objection
2. why the objection makes sense
3. product explanation/demo
4. evidence
5. resolution
6. CTA

### D. Micro-confession
1. “I kept doing X.”
2. specific failed behavior
3. realization
4. changed mechanism/product
5. concrete improvement
6. CTA

### E. Four-panel compression
1. Hook / pain
2. Escalation / old way
3. Product mechanism / turn
4. Payoff + CTA

## Four-panel rule

A 4-panel ad is not a six-panel story with two panels removed. It must be written for compression.

Suggested copy budget:
- panel 1: one short thought
- panel 2: one short thought + optional SFX
- panel 3: one product/mechanism line
- panel 4: one payoff line + compact CTA

## Six-panel rule

Use six panels only if the extra beats create understanding or emotion. Typical jobs:

1. hook
2. problem
3. failed response / consequence
4. mechanism reveal
5. proof/payoff
6. CTA

## One-page layout

For a one-page comic, design the eye path first.

- dominant opening panel near the start of reading flow
- one visually dominant product/mechanism moment
- avoid equal-weight grids when one moment should be emphasized
- keep CTA visually separate from dialogue
- do not place crucial copy against busy backgrounds

## Reading direction

Specify one reading direction and enforce it in:

- panel order
- character gaze
- motion direction
- balloon placement
- CTA placement

Do not mix Japanese right-to-left manga logic with left-to-right ad-card logic accidentally.

## Camera language

Vary shots to preserve pace:

- establishing shot for context only when needed
- medium shot for interaction/product use
- close-up for emotional recognition or proof detail
- insert shot for UI/product evidence

Avoid six consecutive talking-head panels.

## Product reveal timing

The product should appear when the story earns it. Too early can feel like an ad before recognition forms; too late can make the comic memorable but the product forgettable.

## Emotion

Use visible emotional transitions instead of narration explaining emotions. Body posture, eye line, spacing, and action should carry part of the copy load.
