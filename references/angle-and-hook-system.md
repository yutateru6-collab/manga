# Angle and Hook System

## 1. Angle before wording

An angle is a reason a specific viewer should care. A hook is the first visible expression of that angle.

Bad diversity:
- “Stop wasting time.”
- “Don't waste time anymore.”
- “Tired of wasting time?”

Useful diversity:
- time-loss frustration
- fear of forgetting
- identity (“for people who hate ordinary study apps”)
- proof/demo-first
- comparison with the old workflow
- objection handling
- founder/origin problem
- curiosity around a surprising mechanism

## 2. Build the matrix

For each concept fill:

| # | Segment/situation | Motivation | Hook move | First-panel visual | Promise | Mechanism | Proof | CTA | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Aim for 5–10 rows spanning multiple motivations.

## 3. Hook moves

Use a mix of:

- **Recognition:** “This exact situation is me.”
- **Contrast:** visibly compare old/new states.
- **Proof-first:** lead with a genuine result/demo artifact.
- **Confession:** a specific mistake or failed habit.
- **Question:** the exact question the audience actually asks.
- **Curiosity gap:** withhold one piece briefly, then pay it off quickly.
- **Objection-first:** begin with the reason people resist the product/category.
- **Micro-drama:** show a small failure with immediate stakes.
- **Unexpected mechanism:** the product works differently than assumed.
- **Identity:** signal a narrow group/situation clearly.

Avoid empty shock, unrelated spectacle, or a hook the rest of the comic cannot fulfill.

## 4. Visual + copy should divide the work

For static/comic hooks, treat the first panel as two channels:

- **visual information** — what the viewer understands before reading
- **text information** — the sentence that adds the missing thought

Do not make the text merely describe the picture.

Weak:
- image: student staring at 100 screenshots
- text: “I have too many screenshots.”

Stronger:
- image: phone gallery full of vocabulary screenshots
- text: “保存した瞬間だけ、勉強した気になる。”

The image shows the state; the copy supplies the insight.

## 5. Heuristic concept score

This score is a decision aid, not objective truth.

Rate 0–5:

- **Evidence:** how well the angle is grounded in real facts/language
- **Audience recognition:** how quickly the target sees themselves
- **Visualizability:** how naturally the angle becomes a strong panel sequence
- **Mechanism clarity:** whether the product's role can be shown concretely
- **Distinctiveness:** whether it tests a meaningfully different motivation
- **CTA fit:** whether the story naturally leads to the desired action

Subtract 0–5 for:

- **Claim risk:** unsupported/regulated/ambiguous assertions
- **Complexity tax:** too much context needed for a fast social read

Suggested calculation:

`total = evidence + recognition + visualizability + mechanism + distinctiveness + CTA - claim_risk - complexity_tax`

Do not blindly select the numeric winner. If two scores are close, prefer producing both when they test different motivations.

## 6. Funnel diagnosis for existing creatives

If performance data exists, diagnose before rewriting everything:

- low initial engagement → likely hook/first-panel problem
- good first-panel engagement but weak continuation → premise-to-story bridge problem
- readers finish but do not click/act → benefit/proof/offer/CTA problem
- clicks but poor conversion → possible message-to-landing-page mismatch, not necessarily manga quality

Never infer causality from one metric alone when multiple explanations fit.

## 7. Testing discipline

When iterating, change one major variable when possible:

- same story, new hook
- same hook, stronger proof
- same angle, shorter copy
- same copy, clearer visual
- same concept, different character/audience representation

This makes results easier to interpret than replacing the entire ad every round.
